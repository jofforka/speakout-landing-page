Research & Learning

Evidence that strengthens school and youth wellbeing programs.

SpeakOut uses structured learning, feedback and program evidence to improve how we support schools, young people, families and partners. Our research work is designed to inform practical action rather than publish claims we cannot verify.

What we study

  • School wellbeing: student stress, school climate, support structures and teacher readiness.
  • Youth mental health: awareness, stigma, help-seeking, pressure and resilience.
  • Program learning: participation, engagement, feedback, school adoption and implementation quality.
  • Digital access: how young people use wellbeing resources, learning tools and media.

How evidence is collected

Depending on the program, SpeakOut may use needs assessments, anonymous or aggregated surveys, attendance records, structured feedback, facilitator observations, school reports and approved research collaborations. We aim to collect only information that is relevant to the stated purpose.

Responsible research

Work involving children, young people or sensitive wellbeing topics should be designed with safeguarding, privacy, informed participation and appropriate school or adult oversight in mind. Personally identifying or sensitive information should not be published as public impact evidence without an appropriate basis and safeguards.

Reports and public evidence

Public reports, policy briefs, case studies and survey findings will be published only when they are complete enough to be useful and have been reviewed for accuracy, privacy and safeguarding. Until then, this page describes our active research and learning framework rather than displaying placeholder reports.

Work with SpeakOut

Schools, researchers, funders and institutions interested in a research collaboration, data request or program-learning partnership can contact SpeakOut. Requests are reviewed based on purpose, privacy, safeguarding and practical feasibility.
